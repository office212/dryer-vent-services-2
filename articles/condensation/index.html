Water Under Your Dryer? The Hidden Condensation Problem Explained

You walk into your laundry room and find a puddle of water under your dryer. Your first instinct is to call a costly appliance repair technician, assuming the machine is broken or the washer is leaking. But more often than not, the culprit is a clogged dryer vent.

Where is the Water Coming From?

A typical load of wet laundry contains about half a gallon of water. The dryer's job is to heat up that water, turn it into steam, and push it completely out of your home through the exhaust vent.

The Greenhouse Effect

When your vent is blocked by heavy lint buildup or a crushed hose, that hot steam has nowhere to go. It gets trapped inside the pipe. As the steam touches the cooler walls of the ductwork, it quickly turns back into liquid water—a process called condensation. This liquid water runs back down the pipe, pooling inside the machine and eventually leaking out onto your floor.

The Hidden Danger of Mold

A puddle on the floor is annoying, but it's only the visible part of the problem. If water is leaking into your laundry room, it is also likely pooling inside your walls and ceiling where the ductwork runs. Over time, this constant moisture creates the perfect breeding ground for toxic black mold and severe wood rot.
