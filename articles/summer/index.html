Essential Summer Maintenance Tips for Your Dryer Vent

When summer arrives in Staten Island and New Jersey, we rely heavily on our air conditioning to keep the heat out. But did you know that a clogged dryer vent is actively fighting against your AC, driving up your energy bills and increasing fire risks?

The AC vs. Dryer Battle

A clothes dryer generates intense heat. If the exhaust vent is clogged with lint, that hot air cannot escape outside. Instead, it radiates out from the dryer machine and heats up your entire laundry room and surrounding areas.

Your home's HVAC system has to work twice as hard to cool down that excess heat, drastically increasing your monthly electricity bill. A clean vent ensures the heat goes exactly where it should: outside.

Beach Towels and Sand: The Summer Blockage

Summer means beach trips, swimming pools, and lots of heavy towels. Thick beach towels produce significantly more lint than standard clothing. Additionally, sand trapped in bathing suits can mix with the lint, creating a dense, heavy blockage in your ductwork that restricts airflow much faster than normal.

Exterior Vent Cap Inspections

Summer is also peak season for yard work. It is very common for exterior dryer vent caps (the flappers on the outside of your house) to become blocked by overgrown bushes, weed whacker debris, or tall grass. If the flapper cannot open fully, the hot air is trapped inside. Ensure the area around your exterior vent is trimmed and clear.
